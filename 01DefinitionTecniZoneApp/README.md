# `Problem` 

```
Currently, TECNIZONE manages its sales and technical services
operations using static Excel spreadsheets and manually filled
templates, an outdated methodology that negatively impacts the
business’s efficiency. This operational approach generates
several critical deficiencies, including constant data
```

```
duplication, manual calculation of costs, and disorganization
in spare parts inventory, as stock levels are tracked without
low-unit alerts. Furthermore, there is a lack of clear
communication with customers regarding repair progress, as
well as an inefficient registration of physical warranty
checkmarks during reception. Finally, the dispersion of
information across separate files prevents the simple
consolidation of monthly repair counts and collects revenue,
making the development of a centralized application essential
to record device entries, track job statuses, manage basic
spare parts alerts, and offer simple status lookups for
customers.
```

# `Overview` 

```
In the commercial and technical services sector, companies
receive equipment, perform repairs, sell spare parts, and
address customer inquiries every day. When these processes are
managed using text-based tools on a computer, daily work
becomes very difficult and disorganized. For a business to
function well, all information needs to be organized in one
place. This allows them to know exactly which equipment is
coming in, the status of repairs, what spare parts are needed,
and how to respond to customers when they inquire about their
devices.
```

# `Background` 

```
In the domain of commercial operations and technical support
services, business entities daily manage equipment reception,
diagnostic procedures, repair execution, spare parts sales,
and customer inquiry resolution. When these operational
workflows are managed using static text files or standalone
```

```
Excel spreadsheets, daily tasks become highly disorganized and
inefficient. Traditional manual logging methods in workshops
like TECNIZONE produce critical deficiencies, including
repetitive data duplication, manual calculation of repair
costs, and disorganization in spare parts inventory due to the
lack of low-stock alerts. Furthermore, there is an absence of
clear communication channels for customers regarding repair
progress, as well as inefficient registration of physical
warranty checkmarks during equipment reception. To achieve
operational efficiency, all business data must be consolidated
into a single centralized system that accurately records
incoming equipment, repair job statuses, inventory
requirements, and overall financial performance.
```

```
The centralization of information through a dedicated
application fundamentally transforms the operational workflow.
Upon equipment arrival, detailed technical information—
including device type, brand, model, serial number, and
reported faults—is systematically captured and saved directly
into a persistent database to uniquely identify each job.
Additionally, during registration, physical warranty validity
is verified using simple checklist controls, and an estimated
delivery date is selected via a visual calendar interface,
ensuring full traceability and establishing transparent
commitments with the customer from the outset.
```

```
During the technical service lifecycle, technicians update and
track job progress manually using a static dropdown menu
containing predefined status options such as Under Diagnosis,
Under Repair, or Listed. Simultaneously, staff members can
review and filter previous job records using either the
customer’s name or the device serial number. On the financial
and inventory management side, the system allows manual entry
of labor and spare parts costs to automatically display the
total charge on screen, while actively monitoring spare parts
inventory levels and displaying text alerts whenever available
stock drops below 3 units.
```

```
Finally, information integration resolves data dispersion and
enhances both customer satisfaction and business analytics.
Customers can independently look up the real-time status of
their equipment on a simple search screen by entering their
order number, eliminating communication gaps and reducing
manual inquiry handling. Furthermore, the centralized
```

```
repository enables the generation of plain text reports or
simple summary lists displaying the total money collected and
the number of completed repairs per month, providing
management with a clear, consolidated view of TECNIZONE's
operational and financial performance.
```

# `Analyst Comparison` 

```
To evaluate and measure operational efficiency within
TECNIZONE, the system tracks key performance metrics derived
from completed repair cycles, cost structures, and technical
service workflows. Under the traditional operational model,
managing technical tasks and sales with static Excel
spreadsheets severely hinders performance evaluation due to
fragmented records and manual data entry errors. The
centralized application addresses these limitations by
consolidating all service records into a single persistent
database, allowing management to quantitatively analyze repair
throughput, accuracy in financial reporting, and the overall
stability of shop operations.
```

```
The core of the system's evaluation framework relies on
comparing the efficiency of manual logging against automated
data handling through specific operational indicators. The
system tracks the Job Completion Volume (JCV) by automatically
logging every device entry, status change, and completed
service, ensuring that no repair history is lost or
duplicated. Additionally, financial consistency is measured
using the Average Revenue Per Repair (ARPR), which eliminates
manual calculation errors by requiring technicians to input
labor and spare parts costs directly into the system,
automatically computing and displaying the exact total on
screen prior to job closure.
```

```
Furthermore, the system improves operational reliability
through proactive inventory management and streamlined
customer communication. The Inventory Efficiency Index (IEI)
measures the reduction of service delays by monitoring spare
parts stock in real time and triggering instant text alerts
whenever stock levels drop below 3 units, preventing
unexpected component shortages. Complementing this, Service
Status Traceability (SST) evaluates customer support
efficiency by utilizing standardized status options (Under
Diagnosis, Under Repair, Listed) and enabling an order number
```

```
lookup tool for customers, significantly reducing unnecessary
status inquiries and optimizing overall repair delivery.
```
